# Joel's Voice — Style Notes

Notes derived from Joel's rewrite of the site copy (draft 1 → 1.1).
Any future drafting or editing of prose in this project should match
this profile. Joel owns final wording; edits fix mechanics, not voice.

## Stance & tone

- **Collaborative, not oracular.** "We" and "let's" over commanding
  "you" ("Let's look at some common examples" vs "Let me show you
  where"). The reader is a companion, not an audience.
- **Warm and openly enthusiastic.** Exclamation points are welcome
  ("This is the fun part!", "I'd love to talk!"). Playful asides
  ("think the last step was fun?"). Genuine affection stated plainly
  ("the awesome company where I work").
- **Hedged rather than absolute.** "It may seem surprising", "might be
  tempted", "often it's more complex" — softens claims instead of
  punching them. Avoids the confident aphorism-as-argument move.
- **Explains rather than epigrams.** Prefers a plain, complete
  statement over a quotable compression: "Hold part of a system still
  so you can work with the rest of it" replaced "Hold something still,
  and you can trust what moves." Rhetorical polish that calls
  attention to itself gets sanded off.

## Sentence & paragraph mechanics

- **Two spaces after every sentence.** Consistent and deliberate.
- **Full sentences over fragments.** Claude-style staccato fragments
  ("Just one. Ignore everything else.") get expanded into flowing
  sentences with commas. Longer, more conversational rhythm.
- **Fewer em-dash asides.** Parenthetical "— like this —" nesting is
  reduced; ideas get their own sentences instead. When dashes appear,
  spacing/style is casual (sometimes `--`), not typographically fussy.
- **Underscores for italics** (`_datum_`), asterisks for bold.
- **Lists for procedures.** Step-like prose becomes actual bullets
  (the mise en place steps).

## Content instincts

- **Concrete craftsman detail.** Adds practical texture that shows
  real experience: "If it wobbles, shim it so it's stable." Prefers
  the specific mechanic over the abstract summary.
- **Keeps examples inside their domain.** Cookie example uses cookie
  ingredients (chocolate chips, not onions).
- **Prose over tables.** The three-domain comparison table was cut;
  the narrative carries the parallel instead.
- **Questions as transitions.** "Notice the invariants?" — engages the
  reader directly rather than announcing what they will observe.
- **Big claims stated casually**, not portentously: "Savvy builders
  have used it for millennia."

## What to avoid (recognizable Claude tells)

- Aphoristic mic-drop sentences and chiasmus ("the thing you refuse to
  change is the thing that lets you check the thing you did change").
- Staccato fragment runs for emphasis.
- Heavy em-dash–nested qualifications.
- "Not X — it's Y" constructions in rapid succession.
- Announcing the reader's experience ("Notice what happened.",
  "And here is the tempting move").
